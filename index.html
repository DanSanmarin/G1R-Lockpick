Solution

R turn right  ·  L turn left  ·  read left to right
Reading a lock in three minutes
  1. Open the lock in game. Note where every pin starts and enter those positions above.
  2. Turn plate 1 one step to the right. Note which other pins came along, and whether they went right (same) or left (opposite). Turn plate 1 back left to undo — turning costs nothing, only a blocked turn strains the pick.
  3. Repeat for every plate and fill in its own row. Never copy a row from another one: a coupling can run one way only. If a pin sits at hole 1 or 7 and cannot be turned outward, test that plate the other way and press ⇆ on its row to flip what you saw.
  4. Put the pins back where they started, re-check the positions above, then read the sequence: 3R means plate 3, one turn right. Click any turn to see the lock at that point; ← and → step through it.

The rules this solver follows. A lock has three to seven plates; each pin rests in one of seven holes, and the lock springs open when every pin is in hole 4 at the same time. Turning a plate moves its own pin one hole and drags each coupled pin one hole, in the same or the opposite direction. A coupling may be mutual or one-way — plate 2 dragging plate 5 says nothing about what plate 5 does to plate 2 — so every row is recorded from its own test. No pin may be pushed past hole 1 or hole 7: that is the move that strains the pick, so the solver never offers one. Raising the Lockpicking skill gives the pick more durability and removes couplings, which makes a lock simpler — re-read the rows after you train.
